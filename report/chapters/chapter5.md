# CHAPTER FIVE

# SUMMARY, CONCLUSION, CONTRIBUTION TO KNOWLEDGE, AND RECOMMENDATIONS

## 5.1 Summary of the Study

This study set out to design, implement, and evaluate an AI-based attendance system that uses face recognition together with a liveness check, built from proven pretrained components rather than a model trained from scratch, and to report on it honestly, including its defects and limitations, rather than only its best-case behavior. A consent-based enrollment pipeline, a distance-threshold recognition and attendance-logging pipeline, a three-step challenge-response liveness check, an offline evaluation harness computing standard face-verification and face-recognition-specific metrics, and an administrative dashboard with a printable attendance sheet were all designed, implemented, and tested against six real, consenting enrolled participants. Testing under realistic and adversarial conditions surfaced five genuine defects during development, each of which is documented in this report together with the evidence that it was actually fixed, rather than only asserted to be fixed.

## 5.2 Conclusion

Revisiting the aim, objectives, and research questions set out at the start of this study, and answering each against what was actually built and measured:

The aim, to design, implement, and evaluate a face-recognition attendance system with a liveness check, was achieved. All six objectives were met, with one qualified. The enrollment pipeline (objective i) was implemented and used to enroll six real people with 48 accepted photographs. The recognition and attendance-logging pipeline (objective ii) was implemented, verified to correctly reject a genuinely unenrolled participant once a real threshold-configuration defect was found and corrected, and verified to prevent duplicate attendance records at the database level. The challenge-response liveness mechanism (objective iii) was implemented as a three-step sequence and verified to reject a static photograph replay. The evaluation metrics (objective iv) were computed against the real enrolled dataset rather than simulated data, yielding an Accuracy of 94.88%, an FAR of 0.60%, an FRR of 24.84%, and an EER of 11.73%. Realistic-condition testing (objective v) was carried out for no-face, multiple-face, poor-lighting, and anti-spoofing cases, but not for a dedicated, isolated off-angle recognition test, which is reported as an incomplete objective rather than glossed over. The administrative dashboard (objective vi), including the printable attendance sheet, was implemented and used to review real attendance records.

On the research questions: a face recognition pipeline built entirely from pretrained components can distinguish enrolled from unenrolled individuals reasonably well but not perfectly, and only once its decision threshold is deliberately measured and tuned against real data rather than assumed, as the false-accept defect in this study's own testing demonstrated directly (research question i). The system's measured Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, FAR, FRR, and EER are reported in full in the results chapter rather than selectively (research question ii). A lightweight challenge-response liveness check did meaningfully reject the specific static-photograph-replay attack tested in this study, but the fuller three-step version of that check, extended during development at the request of the project's own stakeholders to make each instruction clearer to the person taking attendance, does add a real and non-trivial amount of time per attendance attempt compared with the single-step version originally implemented, so the answer to whether this comes "without excessive slowdown" depends on which version of the mechanism is being asked about (research question iii). Five practical engineering defects were found through testing and are documented individually with before-and-after evidence, spanning an authentication library incompatibility, a database query inefficiency, a server concurrency defect, a threshold miscalibration that caused a real false acceptance, and a liveness-detection constant that was found to be wrong before it caused a failure in production (research question iv).

## 5.3 Contribution to Knowledge

This study's contribution is not a new face recognition algorithm; none was proposed or claimed. Its contribution is a transparent, end-to-end case study of integrating pretrained face recognition and basic liveness detection into a single evaluated attendance system, reporting real measured error rates rather than an idealized description, and documenting the specific engineering defects that arose during real integration and testing together with how each was actually diagnosed and fixed. The identified research gap in the reviewed literature, that attendance-focused studies report recognition accuracy without any liveness check while liveness-focused studies are not evaluated as part of a complete attendance workflow, was addressed directly by building and evaluating both together rather than in isolation.

## 5.4 Recommendations

For this system specifically, three improvements follow directly from the limitations documented in this report. First, the enrolled and impostor test population should be expanded, including a dedicated cohort of people who are deliberately never enrolled, to produce a False Acceptance Rate that reflects genuine strangers rather than only cross-comparisons among a small enrolled group. Second, the liveness detection thresholds, particularly the Eye Aspect Ratio blink threshold and the head-yaw movement threshold, should be recalibrated against real burst-capture video from a larger number of participants rather than the single still photograph used to set the current provisional values. Third, a dedicated off-angle recognition test, systematically varying capture angle rather than relying on the incidental angle variation produced by the liveness check, should be added to close the testing gap identified in objective v.

More broadly, any future work building on this system should continue to treat its threshold and liveness parameters as things to measure and recalibrate against real data specific to the deployment's own cameras, lighting, and population, rather than values to be copied unchanged from this report, since this study's own results showed that even a plausible, literature-informed default value can be wrong until it is actually tested.
