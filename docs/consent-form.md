# Biometric Data Collection Consent Form

**Project:** AI-Based Attendance System Using Face Recognition (Final Year Project)
**Researchers:** Fagbami Michael Oluwapelumi (220903094), Oriji Boluwatife Oluwadamilare (2209030150)
**Supervisor:** Mr. Adetolaju

## What we are asking you to do

We are asking you to allow us to take 5-8 photographs of your face to enroll you in a
prototype face-recognition attendance system, built as a Final Year Project. Later, we
may also ask you to use the system's camera capture (kiosk) so we can test whether it
correctly recognizes you.

## What data is collected and how it is used

- Your enrollment photographs.
- A face embedding derived from those photographs (a set of 128 numbers describing your
  face geometrically; it is not a photograph and cannot be viewed as an image, but it is
  still biometric data specific to you).
- Timestamps of any attendance records generated during testing.

This data is used only to build and evaluate this Final Year Project. It is not shared
with any third party, not used for any purpose beyond this project, and not uploaded to
any public repository or dataset.

## How your data is stored

- Enrollment photographs and face embeddings are stored on the researchers' own
  development machine and in a private, access-controlled database used only by this
  project.
- This data is explicitly excluded from the project's public source code repository.

## Your rights

- Participation is voluntary. You may decline to participate with no consequence.
- You may withdraw your consent at any time, before or after enrollment. If you
  withdraw, your photographs, embeddings, and any attendance records will be deleted.
- You may ask to see what data has been collected about you.
- You may ask questions about this project at any time before, during, or after
  enrollment.

## Known limitations you should be aware of

Face recognition systems are documented in independent research (including NIST's Face
Recognition Vendor Test program) to perform less accurately for some lighting
conditions, skin tones, and demographic groups than others. This is a student prototype
evaluated on a small dataset; its accuracy has not been validated at a scale that would
support claims of fairness or reliability across all groups.
